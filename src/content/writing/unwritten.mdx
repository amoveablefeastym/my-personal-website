<div class="not-prose font-serif text-[#444] dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed space-y-8 text-lg">

<p>This screenplay started with a feeling I couldn't shake: that so many women are carrying stories that don't have anywhere to go. We shape our lives around what we can't say, around other people's expectations, around the fear that telling the truth will change something we're not ready to change. <em>Unwritten</em> is about that feeling. About the silences that don't look dramatic on the outside, but still shape everything underneath.</p>

<p>I've always been interested in authorship. Not just who's speaking, but who gets to speak. Who gets to tell the story, and in whose voice. This film explores what happens when that voice is borrowed. When someone writes their life through someone else's name because they can't bear to speak as themselves. And when someone else, years later, is left with the pieces and has to decide what to do with them.</p>

<p>One of the things that matters most to me in this story is the way women relate to each other. Not in loud confrontations or clear rivalries, but in those tense, quiet moments of recognition. The entanglement between the wife and the ex-girlfriend, the mother and the daughter, the friend who sees through you and calls you out anyway. These relationships are rarely simple. There's jealousy, admiration, fear, projection, care, intimacy. What interests me is how these women look at each other, and what they see. And how sometimes, in places we didn't expect, we find the most devastating kind of care: the feeling of being seen by someone who was never meant to see you.</p>

</div>

<div class="not-prose w-16 h-px bg-gray-300 dark:bg-gray-700 mx-auto my-12"></div>

<div class="not-prose font-serif text-[#444] dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed space-y-8 text-lg">

<p>For Elena, choosing marriage and motherhood meant giving up parts of herself. Or maybe not giving them up entirely, just tucking them away. Then she catches a glimpse of another life, Rebecca's life, full of movement and freedom. At first, she thinks she chose wrong. That she settled. That she lost. But when she really sees Rebecca, what she sees isn't someone untouched by consequence, but someone who has made her own hard choices and is living fully inside them. Someone who didn't run from responsibility, but claimed it on her own terms. That moment of recognition is everything. It doesn't undo Elena's choices, but it changes how she understands them.</p>

<p>For Mira, the story is different. She's standing at that fragile moment right before adult life begins, where every decision feels like it could determine who she becomes. She wants to get it right. She wants to be prepared. She hides inside theory and structure, trying to stay safe. Right now she's facing the choice of whether to turn her mother's story and the trauma she experienced into clean academic language, or to let it remain something messy and alive. Whether to name it. Whether to feel it.</p>

<p>Elena's absence is the wound Mira tried to rationalize and outgrow. At first, she's angry. Then she tries to understand. But what finally matters is that she begins to feel the full weight of it, not as abandonment, but as a choice her mother made after years of being silent. A choice that hurt, yes, but also came from a deep need to stop disappearing inside her own life. Mira may never fully agree with it, but she stops trying to explain it away. She learns to carry it without letting it define her. And that is its own kind of forgiveness.</p>

<p>And then there's Leah. The person who challenges Mira without forcing her. The one who sees her patterns and still sticks around. She's a character I love deeply, not because she has all the answers, but because she insists on showing up. She's honest. She's emotionally present. She pushes, but not cruelly. She lives in contradiction and still takes up space. And through her, Mira begins to see herself more clearly, and understand that intimacy means letting someone see you even when you don't know what you're becoming yet.</p>

</div>

<div class="not-prose w-16 h-px bg-gray-300 dark:bg-gray-700 mx-auto my-12"></div>

<div class="not-prose font-serif text-[#444] dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed space-y-8 text-lg">

<p>This isn't a story about revelation. It's a story about what happens after you know. When you are sitting with the truth, and you have to decide what kind of person you want to be in the face of it. Whether you stay quiet, or take the risk of saying something real.</p>

<p>It's about women who wrote in borrowed voices. Women who stayed silent so they wouldn't be left. Women who watched each other's lives and didn't always know what to do with that tenderness. Women who tried their best.</p>

<p>It's about how complicated it is to choose, not once, but over and over. And how freedom, in the end, isn't escaping or floating. It's the willingness to act without guarantees. To take responsibility for what we have chosen, and for a life that's still unfolding.</p>

<p>It's about what we do with what we inherit. Whether we keep carrying it, or finally put it down.</p>

</div>
