A quarter-long collaboration with Shopify to investigate: *How might Shopify use AI to help college students launch and run small businesses, reducing friction from idea to first revenue?*

### The Problem

Shopify has 80% brand awareness among Gen Z, but only 7.3% of its merchant base is 18-24. That gap isn't because students don't want to build things. We surveyed 58 students and found that 29% were already doing something entrepreneurial (tutoring, reselling, freelancing) but only 9% called it that. The barrier wasn't access to tools. It was identity: students didn't see Shopify as something built for people like them, and most got stuck before they ever made a website.

### What We Did

We ran the full research-to-prototype cycle. We designed and distributed surveys (100+ responses), conducted 7 user interviews, synthesized findings into personas and journey maps, and identified exactly where students drop off on the path from "wants to make money" to first sale. Most never got past having an idea.

From there we moved into Figma with several rounds of high-fidelity prototyping, each followed by user testing (21 sessions total).

The final prototype was a polished mockup featuring a browsable student store gallery, real founder revenue data, a "Clone This Store" duplication feature powered by Shopify's Sidekick AI, and a free-until-first-sale pricing model. We presented the full research, prototype, and strategic recommendations directly to Shopify in a final client presentation.

The strongest reactions weren't about features. They were about recognition. Seeing someone who looked like them, running a business that looked achievable, was what moved people from passive to interested.

The version of Shopify we were designing toward isn't just a platform. It's a student entrepreneurship ecosystem that grows itself, where every founder who succeeds makes it a little easier for the next one.
