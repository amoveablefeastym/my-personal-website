*Figbuild 2026 · 72-hour design challenge · Team of 3 (with Jay Jeon and Elisa Huang)*

Boredom is a physical experience, but nobody tracks it. Students sit through lectures, feel their attention slip, and instinctively reach for their phones. The doom-scrolling kicks in. The boredom gets worse. Existing tools like Apple Health or Screen Time show you what happened, but not why. They can't connect physical disengagement to digital behavior or tell you when a boredom spiral is actually starting.

### What We Designed

Drift detects boredom by fusing two streams of data: digital activity (tab switching, scroll speed, phone pickup frequency) and physical signals (heart rate variability, eye movement, fidgeting). When the combination hits a threshold, a "boredom spiral detected" alert surfaces a context-aware intervention. It asks what you're doing right now, then suggests a science-backed micro-action tailored to that moment: switching to diagrams mid-lecture, setting a microgoal before the next slide, or chewing gum to restore focus.

Beyond real-time intervention, the app gives users a boredom timeline: a dashboard showing when boredom reliably hits, what triggers it, and how long it compounds. The goal isn't just to interrupt the spiral. It's to help users actually understand their attention patterns over time.

### How We Built It

All in Figma. We designed the full end-to-end experience: onboarding (including a deliberate white-to-purple reveal moment as users enter the Drift aesthetic), a granular permissions flow that separates each data type individually for transparency rather than bundling them into one ask, a data syncing screen, the boredom timeline dashboard, and the live intervention overlay. The design system, component library, and all screens were built from scratch in 72 hours.

Every UX decision had a rationale. The three-option intervention cards were chunked for memory load, the permissions screen included expandable privacy details because the app handles sensitive biometric data, and the onboarding copy was written to reframe boredom as something trackable rather than something shameful.
